# Drum beats

The Device's drum beats (Shift + Drum grid) are 81 grooves in ten styles, from a kick and a hi-hat to Ravel's _Boléro_ and the tabla's taals. The red knob picks a style and the blue knob a beat. A tap or Play previews it on its own kit and tempo, and Save loads it into the drum grid, setting the tempo and time signature to match.

Most are the common forms drummers teach rather than transcriptions: written out simply, for the Device's kits, to be played and changed. Where a groove comes from one record or one drummer, its entry names them, and the examples say where to hear the feel. All credit for the grooves goes to the drummers who made them.

## Reading the grids

Each row is a piece of the kit and each character a step of the drum grid; spaces mark the beats.

| Mark | Means                     |
| ---- | ------------------------- |
| `X`  | accented hit              |
| `x`  | hit                       |
| `o`  | ghost note, played softly |
| `.`  | rest                      |

Most beats count sixteenths, four steps a beat. Swung ones use triplets, three a beat, or six steps a beat for swung sixteenths. The 12/8 and 6/8 beats count eighths, two steps each, grouped by the dotted quarter. Tempos count the meter's beat: quarters in 4/4, eighths in 6/8 and 12/8. The tabla's taals are written as their bols, one per mātrā (the taal's own beat), with `|` between its sections.

## Simple

Starting points: a kick and a hi-hat, and a snare in the last few.

### Boom tss

Drum kit · 90 BPM · 4/4 · sixteenths

```text
Closed hat  .... x... .... x...
Kick        x... .... x... ....
```

The plainest groove: the kick on 1 and 3, the hat answering on 2 and 4. Boom, tss, boom, tss.

### Kick kick hat

Drum kit · 90 BPM · 4/4 · sixteenths

```text
Closed hat  .... x... .... x...
Kick        x... .... x.x. ....
```

Kick, hat, kick-kick, hat: the kick on 1, the hat on 2, two kicks on 3 and the and after it, and the hat on 4.

### Offbeat hat

Drum kit · 120 BPM · 4/4 · sixteenths

```text
Closed hat  ..x. ..x. ..x. ..x.
Kick        x... x... x... x...
```

The kick on every beat and the hat on every and between them: four on the floor at its simplest.

### Eighth hats

Drum kit · 100 BPM · 4/4 · sixteenths

```text
Closed hat  x.x. x.x. x.x. x.x.
Kick        x... .... x... ....
```

The kick on 1 and 3 under eighths on the hat, with no snare: a pulse to build on.

### Kick and snare

Drum kit · 96 BPM · 4/4 · sixteenths

```text

Kick   x... .... x... ....
```

The bare backbeat: the kick on 1 and 3 and the snare on 2 and 4, nothing else.

## Rock

Backbeats on the snare, eighths on the hats or a cymbal, and the kick pushing the bar along.

### Rock

Rock kit · 116 BPM · 4/4 · sixteenths

```text
Crash       X... .... .... ....
Closed hat  x.x. x.x. x.x. x.x.
Snare       .... X... .... X...
Kick        x... .... x.x. ....
```

The backbone of rock: eighths on the hat, the kick on 1 and 3 with a push on the and of 3, the snare cracking on 2 and 4, and a crash to open the bar.

_Examples:_ "Back in Black" and "Highway to Hell" (AC/DC, Phil Rudd)

### Hard rock

Rock kit · 110 BPM · 4/4 · sixteenths

```text
Ride bell  x.x. x.x. x.x. x.x.
Snare      .... X... .... X...
Kick       x.x. .... x.x. ....
```

The same frame made heavier: the kick doubled on 1 and 3, and the eighths moved to the ride's bell to cut through loud guitars.

### Punk

Rock kit · 180 BPM · 4/4 · sixteenths

```text
Crash       X... .... .... ....
Closed hat  x... x... x... x...
Snare       ..X. ..X. ..X. ..X.
Kick        x... x... x... x...
```

The skank beat: the kick on every beat and the snare on every and, an oom-pah driven hard and fast.

_Examples:_ Skate punk, from Bad Religion to NOFX

### Half-time

```text

Kick        x... .... ..x. ....
```

The snare moves from 2 and 4 to 3 alone, so the bar feels twice as long while the hats keep the tempo: the weight of a breakdown or a heavy chorus.

### Bo Diddley

```text

```

The 3-2 son clave squeezed into one bar, played on the floor tom over a wash of maracas: shave-and-a-haircut as a groove.

_Examples:_ "Bo Diddley" (Bo Diddley), "Not Fade Away" (Buddy Holly), "I Want Candy" (The Strangeloves), "Faith" (George Michael)

### Stomp clap

Drum kit · 81 BPM · 4/4 · sixteenths

```text

Kick  x.x. .... x.x. ....
```

Two stomps and a clap, twice a bar, with nothing else: a beat a stadium can play.

_Examples:_ "We Will Rock You" (Queen)

### Half-time shuffle

Drum kit · 86 BPM · 4/4 · triplets

```text
Closed hat  x.x x.x x.x x.x
Snare       .o. .o. Xo. .o.
Kick        x.. ..x ... ..x
```

A shuffle at half time: the hats on the triplet's outside notes, ghost notes on every middle one, and the backbeat only on 3. Jeff Porcaro built it from Bernard Purdie's shuffle and John Bonham's.

_Examples:_ "Rosanna" (Toto, Jeff Porcaro), "Fool in the Rain" (Led Zeppelin, John Bonham)

### Train beat

Jazz kit · 140 BPM · 4/4 · sixteenths

```text
Snare  oooo Xooo oooo Xooo
Kick   x... .... x... ....
```

Brushes churning sixteenths on the snare with the backbeat leaning out, like wheels on a track: the push behind country and rockabilly.

Four slow beats of three: triplets on the hat, the snare on the second and fourth dotted quarter. The rolling feel of soul and doo-wop ballads.

_Examples:_ "At Last" (Etta James), "Everybody Hurts" (R.E.M.)

## Funk and soul

Sixteenths, ghost notes and syncopation: grooves built to be danced to.

_Examples:_ "Cold Sweat" and "Funky Drummer" (James Brown, Clyde Stubblefield)

Bernard Purdie's half-swung shuffle: the hats on the triplet's outside notes, ghost notes on every middle one, the backbeat on 2 and 4.

_Examples:_ "Home at Last" and "Babylon Sisters" (Steely Dan, Bernard Purdie)

Hip hop's drum machines with swung sixteenths and a backbeat doubled by a clap, built for dancing to R&B.

### Afrobeat

Drum kit · 112 BPM · 4/4 · sixteenths

## Jazz

Swung on a triplet grid, time kept on the ride and the hat's foot on 2 and 4, the kick feathered.

### Swing

Jazz kit · 150 BPM · 4/4 · triplets

```text
Ride        x.. x.x x.. x.x
Closed hat  ... x.. ... x..
Kick        o.. o.. o.. o..
```

The ride's ding, ding-a-ding over the hat's foot on 2 and 4, the kick feathered on every beat: time kept on the cymbal rather than the drums.

_Examples:_ "So What" (Miles Davis, Jimmy Cobb)

### Two-feel

Jazz kit · 120 BPM · 4/4 · triplets

```text
Ride         x.. x.x x.. x.x
Closed hat   ... x.. ... x..
Cross-stick  ... ... ... x..
Kick         o.. ... o.. ...
```

Swing with the bass in halves, kick and cross-stick marking only the strong beats: how a tune's head often sits before the solos open up.

### Bebop

Jazz kit · 220 BPM · 4/4 · triplets, 2 bars

```text
Ride        x.. x.x x.. x.x x.. x.x x.. x.x
Closed hat  ... x.. ... x.. ... x.. ... x..
Snare       ..o ... ..o ... ... ..o ... ...
Kick        ... ... ... ... ... ... ... ..X
```

Kenny Clarke and Max Roach moved the time to the ride and freed the snare and kick to comp, dropping bombs between the soloist's phrases.

## Electronic

Dance music's machines and breaks, from four on the floor to the Amen.

## Classical

The dance and march rhythms of the concert hall and the band.

### Waltz

Drum kit · 168 BPM · 3/4 · eighths

```text
Snare  .. x. x.
Kick   x. .. ..
```

Oom-pah-pah: the bass on 1 and the snare on 2 and 3, as the orchestra's low strings and the rest of the band split the bar.

_Examples:_ "The Blue Danube" (Johann Strauss II)

### Polka

Drum kit · 120 BPM · 2/4 · sixteenths

```text
Snare  ..x. ..x.
Kick   x... x...
```

Oom-pah in two: the bass on the beat, the snare on every and.

_Examples:_ "Tritsch-Tratsch-Polka" (Johann Strauss II)

### March

Drum kit · 120 BPM · 2/4 · sixteenths

```text
Crash  X... ....
Snare  x.xx x.x.
Kick   x... x...
```

A marching band's bass drum on the beat and cymbals on the downbeat, the snare filling the bar.

_Examples:_ "The Stars and Stripes Forever" (John Philip Sousa)

### Boléro

Drum kit · 72 BPM · 3/4 · six steps a beat, 2 bars

```text
Snare  x..xxx x..xxx x..x.. x..xxx x..xxx xxxxxx
```

Ravel's two-bar ostinato of eighths and sixteenth triplets, which the snare plays unchanged for the whole piece.

_Examples:_ _Boléro_ (Maurice Ravel)

### Habanera

Drum kit · 72 BPM · 2/4 · sixteenths

```text
Low tom      x..x x.x.
Cross-stick  x... x...
```

A dotted eighth, a sixteenth and two eighths: the Cuban habanera, whose rhythm also runs under the tango.

_Examples:_ "L'amour est un oiseau rebelle" (the Habanera from _Carmen_, Georges Bizet)

## South Asian

The tabla's taals, each played as its theka, the bols that mark its cycle, and Nepali madal rhythms.

### Teentaal

Tabla · 120 BPM · 4/4 · eighths, 4 bars

```text
Dha Dhin Dhin Dha | Dha Dhin Dhin Dha | Dha Tin Tin Na | Na Dhin Dhin Dha
```

The most common Hindustani taal: sixteen beats in four sections of four. Sam, the first beat, carries the weight; the third section opens on khali, the empty beat, where the bayan drops out.

### Keherwa

Tabla · 96 BPM · 4/4 · eighths

```text
Dha Ge Na Te | Na Ke Dhin Na
```

Eight beats in two sections of four, light and quick: the taal of folk songs, bhajans and film music.

### Dadra

Tabla · 200 BPM (in eighths) · 6/8 · sixteenths

```text
Dha Dhin Na | Dha Tin Na
```

Six beats in two sections of three, lilting like a waltz: the taal of light classical songs.

### Rupak

Tabla · 180 BPM (in eighths) · 7/8 · sixteenths

```text
Tin Tin Na | Dhin Na | Dhin Na
```

Seven beats in sections of three, two and two, unusual in starting on khali rather than a strong beat.

### Jhaptaal

Tabla · 132 BPM · 5/4 · eighths, 2 bars

```text
Dhin Na | Dhin Dhin Na | Tin Na | Dhin Dhin Na
```

Ten beats in sections of two, three, two and three, limping and elegant.

### Ektaal

Tabla · 120 BPM (in eighths) · 12/8 · sixteenths

```text
Dhin Dhin | Dha Te | Tun Na | Ke Na | Dha Te | Dhin Na
```

Twelve beats in six sections of two, often played slowly for khayal. Its dhage and tirakita phrases are each sounded as one stroke here.

### Jhyaure

Madal · 210 BPM (in eighths) · 6/8 · sixteenths

```text
Dha Ti Ta | Dhin Ti Ta
```

The lilting six-beat rhythm of Nepali folk song and dance, on the madal.

### Folk four

Madal · 110 BPM · 4/4 · eighths, 2 bars

```text
Dha   x...............
Ti    ..x...x...x...x.
Ta    ....x.......x...
Dhin  ........x.......
Ka    ..............x.
```

A four-beat Nepali folk pattern on the madal.
